When the addressable market contains fewer than a thousand plausible accounts, a broad demand-generation playbook can become a costly distraction.

The problem is not that the audience is too small to market to. It is that the business cannot afford to confuse a company that could theoretically buy with an account it can realistically win and serve. Each unsuitable target consumes research, outreach and sales capacity that cannot be replenished by finding an unlimited new audience.

An ideal customer profile is therefore a working allocation rule, not a decorative persona slide.

## Start with the problem your offer can solve

Industry and employee count are convenient filters. They are rarely enough. Two companies with the same size and sector can have different operational constraints, buying authority and urgency.

Write the conditions that make your offer relevant: the underlying problem, its consequences, the required operating environment and the barriers your team can overcome. Then state exclusions. If a buyer needs capabilities you cannot support, that belongs in the profile before outreach starts.

Separate the account profile from individual buyer roles. The organisation may fit while the contact has no responsibility for the problem. A plausible job title is a route to investigation, not proof of authority.

## Build an account ledger with evidence

Create one record per target organisation, with a stable account identity and supporting evidence. Store why it belongs in the market, the date checked and what remains unknown. A copied list without evidence is inventory, not a strategy.

| Dimension | Evidence to seek |
| --- | --- |
| Problem fit | Public workflow, product or operating constraint |
| Economic fit | A credible relationship between value and buying capacity |
| Delivery fit | Geography, integration and service requirements |
| Buyer access | Identifiable roles responsible for the problem |
| Timing | A relevant change, initiative or expressed evaluation |
| Exclusion | A documented reason the account cannot be served |

Do not assign certainty to inferred facts. Mark a guessed technology stack or buying initiative as unverified. Keep confidential and personal data out of shared campaign labels and limit access to the research ledger.

## Rank accounts without pretending to know everything

Use a simple tiering model that colleagues can explain. A top tier might require strong problem fit and no known delivery barrier; a second tier might fit but lack timing evidence. The labels are less important than reproducibility.

Do not let engagement compensate for a fundamental exclusion. A company that repeatedly reads your content does not become a good customer if its needs remain incompatible with your offer.

HubSpot's distinction between fit and engagement scores provides a useful implementation reference for keeping those dimensions separate. It does not supply the correct profile for your business. [3](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/scoring/understand-the-lead-scoring-tool)

## Connect tiers to different actions

A priority account with clear timing may justify tailored outreach and specialist research. A suitable account without urgency may belong in a slower educational programme. An excluded account should not keep receiving sales attention because it clicked an advertisement.

Write the promised next action for each tier. Assign an owner and a review date. A tiering model that does not change behaviour is simply a new reporting dimension.

Avoid equating a small market with permission to contact everyone aggressively. Channel rules, lawful processing, objections and opt-outs still apply. Relevance improves the argument for a conversation; it does not remove the recipient's choice.

## Evaluate accounts, not only contacts

Several contacts at one organisation can make lead volume look healthy while adding no new account coverage. Track reached accounts, engaged accounts, qualified opportunities and customer outcomes at the appropriate account level.

Keep the denominator visible. How many validated accounts are in each tier? How many were actually reached? How many had enough time to respond? An apparent conversion improvement can result from narrowing the list after seeing the outcome.

Review both successful and rejected accounts. If sales repeatedly finds the same delivery barrier, change the exclusion rules. If unexpected customers succeed, inspect what the current profile overlooked rather than treating them as exceptions forever.

## Treat precision as a maintained system

Accounts change products, ownership, strategy and buying priorities. Set review points around meaningful signals and commercial learning instead of assuming a list remains current indefinitely.

For the qualification loop, read [Lead Scoring Is Only as Good as the Sales Feedback](/growth-systems/lead-scoring-feedback-loop). Precision is not the ambition to know everything about every company. It is the discipline of recording what you know, acting proportionately and correcting the allocation rule when outcomes disagree.
